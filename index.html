01 — About

Modeling water for people and the planet

Fitsume Teshome Wolkeba, Ph.D. is a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Bureau of Economic Geology, The University of Texas at Austin, where he develops data-driven and physically-based approaches to quantify water availability and scarcity across river basins and croplands.

His doctoral and postdoctoral research couples global and continental hydrological models with remote sensing and machine learning to address one of the defining challenges of our time: ensuring water security under a shifting climate. He has quantified scarcity vulnerability across 331 U.S. river basins, exposed how indicator choice and temporal aggregation distort global scarcity estimates, and mapped crop water consumption worldwide.

Before joining UT Austin, Fitsume earned his Ph.D. in Civil, Construction, and Environmental Engineering at the University of Alabama, served as a lecturer and practicing hydrologist in Ethiopia, and contributed to national water-prediction efforts as a fellow at the NOAA National Water Center.

02 — Research Interests

Where my work lives

Five connected threads, from the physics of the water cycle to the algorithms that help us see it more clearly.

≈

Hydrology & Water Resources

Large-scale and process-based modeling of the terrestrial water cycle — streamflow, evapotranspiration, and water availability across basins.

◈

Machine Learning

LSTM and data-driven models for streamflow prediction and data assimilation within next-generation water modeling frameworks.

⬡

Remote Sensing

Satellite and gridded products for precipitation and evapotranspiration, evaluated for fidelity in continental-scale water budgets.

⊞

Data Integration

Fusing observations, reanalysis, and model output — building open tooling that makes heterogeneous hydrologic data usable.

◐

Water Security & Scarcity

Quantifying who is exposed to water scarcity, how robust those estimates are, and how climate change reshapes vulnerability.

⟁

Water Footprint

Spatially explicit blue and green water consumption of global crop production and its trajectory over time.

    Journal of Hydrology, 628

    Quantifies how the choice of gridded precipitation input propagates into water-availability estimates from large-scale hydrologic models, evaluated with Pearson correlation, NSE, percent bias, and Kling–Gupta efficiency.
